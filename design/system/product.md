# Product

The durable context every Cooked design decision starts from. Read this before designing or reviewing any screen.

## What Cooked is

A shared kitchen app. People scan a grocery receipt (or their fridge, pantry, freezer, food or a recipe) and Cooked puts each item where it lives: fridge, pantry or freezer. It keeps track of what's in the kitchen, imports recipes from a link or pasted text, and keeps one shopping list that everyone in the household can add to and check off.

## Who it's for

People who share a kitchen: partners, families and roommates who cook and shop for the same home. Also people who cook alone and want to stop forgetting what's in the fridge.

## The jobs

1. Stock the kitchen without typing: scan a receipt and be done.
2. Know what's there and what needs using soon.
3. Shop together from one list, without buying the same thing twice.
4. Find a recipe and see what's already in the kitchen for it.

## Platform

An iPhone app first (390 × 844 design frames). Native iOS expectations apply: a bottom tab bar, back gestures, Sign in with Apple, the system share sheet.

## Surface mode

Operate. People open Cooked to do something quick in the kitchen or the shop: check, add, tick off, scan. Speed and scanability outrank expression. The brand lives in a few precise details, mainly the sticky notes.

## Tone

Calm, warm and homemade. White and uncluttered, with one playful element: the kitchen shown as tilted paper notes with handwriting. Copy is plain and specific ("Use soon", "2 of 6 done", "Added by Sam").

## Onboarding

One thing per screen, like Instagram's sign-up. Sign-in choices offered side by side, like Airbnb. Onboarding uses sage as its button color.

## Hard rules

- Follow the Cooked design system tokens and components.
- Pass every item in **Avoid the AI look**.
- Text that matters meets 4.5:1 contrast; touch targets are at least 44px.
